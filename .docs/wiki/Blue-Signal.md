# Blue Signal and separate connections

Blue Signal carries an ON/OFF instruction. Electricity carries energy. Item routes carry inventory items, and fluid routes carry stored liquid. Connecting one channel never silently supplies another.

A Lever stays ON or OFF until used again. A Button sends a one-second pulse. A Signal Indicator shows the signal without needing electricity; a Workshop Lamp needs electricity and can also accept optional signal control.

Signal Wire runs horizontally. Signal Conduit can route through all six directions. A Relay has rear input and front output, with a one-tick delay between them.

## Control a machine

Connect a lever through signal wire or conduit to the machine’s **front** signal input. A Pump, Crusher, Drill or Workshop Lamp with no signal connected is allowed to operate when its other requirements are met. An attached OFF signal disables it; ON permits operation. Power still needs its separate input.

Tank Signal Valve Ports behave differently: they require an attached ON signal to open. Configure the fluid port as INPUT or OUTPUT as well. A Tank Level Sensor outputs ON when stored liquid reaches its adjustable percentage threshold.

## Add channels to transport pipes

Open an Item Pipe or Fluid Pipe and choose FIT SIGNAL or FIT POWER. In Survival each fitting costs one Signal Conduit or one Power Cable; Creative can fit them without consuming inventory. Both additions can coexist.

The shape of each channel follows its own actual connections. A fitted fluid route might carry water, a blue control instruction and electricity together, but these remain independent networks. Plain Item Pipe and Fluid Pipe do not carry signal or electricity.

For a tank valve, connect the fluid route to its front nozzle and the signal route to the keyed fitting. Confirm the valve status and input/output mode in its interface. See [tank construction](Tanks.md) and [power generation](Electricity-and-batteries.md).
